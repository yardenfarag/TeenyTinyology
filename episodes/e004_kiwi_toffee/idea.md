# Episode 004 — Kiwi & Toffee (the crab-hacker barnacle)

*Sacculina carcini*, a parasitic barnacle (Rhizocephala, "root-heads"); its host the shore crab / European green crab
*Carcinus maenas*. Owner's idea (2026-11-01, pasted): a parasite that makes a crab raise the parasite's babies, even
if the crab is male. It's a barnacle that looks nothing like one. The female injects an early stage into the crab,
grows a root network inside, absorbs nutrients, suppresses the crab's reproduction, grows an egg sac under the
abdomen where a female would carry eggs; the crab grooms and protects the sac and helps release the larvae; infected
males grow broad female-like abdomens and perform maternal care. It redirects the crab's reproductive effort.

## Cast (Kiwi approved 2026-11-01; "Button" rejected by the owner, renamed Toffee)
- **Kiwi** — shore crab / green crab (*Carcinus maenas*), **male**. New. Green shell with five teeth each side,
  yellow-green underside, orange claw tips, narrow "lighthouse" tail flap that broadens over the episode.
- **Toffee** — crab-hacker barnacle (*Sacculina carcini*), **female**: cypris larva → kentrogon → interna (roots)
  → externa (the "toffee" under Kiwi's tail). New. Cream-brown oval sac with a tiny face near the mantle opening.
  Her tiny dwarf males appear but are not named.

## Hook
Teaser + first line: **"This crab is guarding a clutch of eggs. And they aren't even his."** (owner, 2026-11-01: "his" already says he is male)
(Checked against the hedge column of facts.md: infected males carry and care for the externa — #10, #11.)

## The story in beats
1. **Hook.** Kiwi on a rock in a tide pool, tail flap tucked, something cream-brown under it.
2. **Meet them.** "This is Kiwi. He's a shore crab…" Then Toffee: a speck of a larva drifting in. "She's a barnacle."
   (The surprise: nothing like the barnacles on rocks.)
3. **The needle.** Toffee lands on Kiwi, walks to a soft spot, sheds her shell, becomes a kentrogon with a hollow
   stylet, and injects herself — a soft wormlet — into his blood. (sfx, not spoken.)
4. **Roots.** Inside Kiwi, she grows a network of roots through his body and drinks from his blood. Kiwi looks fine.
   He stops moulting (so stops growing). He won't have babies of his own.
5. **The sac.** Months later, a sac pushes out under his tail flap — exactly where a mother crab carries her eggs.
6. **The change, part 1: his body.** Kiwi's narrow tail flap really grows wide and round, the shape a mother crab
   has; his claws stay small. Scientists think the wide flap shelters the sac (hypothesis, hedged).
7. **Her babies.** Tiny male larvae slip into the sac; it fills with Toffee's eggs.
7b. **The change, part 2: his behaviour.** "Kiwi is still a boy. But now he behaves like a mother." He cleans and
   guards the sac and treats her eggs as his own. (Owner, 2026-11-01: make clear it's both a body change and a
   behaviour change, and that he is not turned into a female.)
8. **The launch.** When the larvae are ready, Kiwi climbs up, bobs and fans — the way a mother crab hatches her eggs —
   and a cloud of Toffee's larvae streams out.
9. **The turn / cycle.** She doesn't just take his food. She takes his whole job of making babies, and hands him
   hers. The sac refills; more broods. The larvae drift off to find the next crab.
   End card: *Sacculina carcini* · the crab-hacker barnacle · *Carcinus maenas*, logo plate.
