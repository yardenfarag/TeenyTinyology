# Episode 005 — Mochi, Pip & Noodle (*Dipylidium caninum*) — fact sheet

Sources:

- **CDC** https://www.cdc.gov/dipylidium/about/index.html — patient page (4–28 in)

- **WP** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipylidium_caninum · **WP-T** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapeworm

| # | Claim | Status | Safe wording | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fresh gravid segments (proglottids, ~12 × 3 mm, cucumber/pumpkin-seed shaped) are motile and are seen around the anus, on the fur, on bedding; dried ones look like grains of rice (~2 mm, hard, yellowish). | ✅ | "a grain of rice… and it's moving"; "a tiny piece of a tapeworm" | CDC-DPDx; CAPC |
| 2 | Segments are full of egg packets (capsules of several eggs, e.g. 8 visible in a CDC image). Segments disintegrate in the environment and release the packets. | ✅ | "packed with eggs… it dries, breaks open, the eggs spill out" | CDC-DPDx; WP |
| 3 | Intermediate host: larvae of the cat/dog flea (*Ctenocephalides*), occasionally the dog louse. | ✅ | the story follows a flea; never "only fleas" | CDC-DPDx; CAPC |
| 4 | Flea larvae live in the host's bedding/environment and eat organic debris, mainly adult flea faeces ("flea dirt"), also eggs. | ✅ | "eats the crumbs he finds" | Merck Vet; fleascience |
| 5 | In the larva's gut the egg hatches (oncosphere), bores through the gut wall into the body cavity and develops; it stays quiescent through the pupa. | ✅ | "the egg hatches… a young worm slips into his body… and waits" | WP / Commons life-cycle; Parasite 2017 |
| 6 | It becomes infective only after the adult flea is on a warm host: skin surface temperature (31–36 °C), not the blood meal, completes development (~1–3 days). | ✅ | "In Mochi's warm fur, the tiny worm inside him gets ready." — never "the blood feeds it" | Pugh 1987; Parasite 2017 |
| 7 | Cats and dogs are infected by swallowing an infected flea while grooming. | ✅ | "Itch… lick… (gulp)" | CDC-DPDx; CAPC |
| 8 | The flea is digested and the young worm is freed in the small intestine; adult attaches with an armed rostellum (rows of hooks) and four suckers. | ✅ | "a crown of tiny hooks and four suckers" | CDC-DPDx; WP |
| 9 | Tapeworms have no mouth or gut; they absorb nutrients through the tegument (skin). | ✅ | "Noodle has no mouth. She soaks up Mochi's food through her skin." | WP-T |
| 10 | Adult length 10–70 cm (CDC), 15–70 cm (CAPC); CDC patient page 4–28 in. | ✅ | "can grow longer than a ruler" (30 cm is inside every range; "can") | CDC-DPDx; CAPC |
| 11 | New segments form behind the scolex; each matures with male and female organs (two sets — "double-pored") and fills with eggs; the oldest, ripest are at the end and detach. | ✅ | "each new segment fills with eggs… the oldest break off" | WP; CAPC |
| 12 | Segments shed from 2–3 weeks after infection (CAPC); CDC: adult after ~1 month. | ⚠️ sources differ | no number spoken; "weeks later" if needed | CAPC; CDC-DPDx |
| 13 | Infections are usually without obvious signs; some animals lick/scoot. Praziquantel/epsiprantel clear it; flea control prevents reinfection. | ✅ | "Most cats barely notice. A vet can clear Noodle out, and stopping fleas stops the next one." | CAPC |
| 14 | People (mostly children) can rarely be infected by swallowing a flea. | ✅ | not used | CDC |

## Hook / closing checklist (can't / only / always / never)
- "There's a grain of rice on this cat's fur. And it's moving." — #1 ✅ (fresh segments move; dried look like rice — the
  caption/teaser shows a moving pale segment; the word "grain" is the look, the next line corrects it).
- "Most cats barely notice" — hedged "most" ✅. "Stopping fleas stops the next one" — fleas are the usual route (#3) ✅.
- "A cat. A flea. A worm. Round and round." — no rule claimed ✅.

Not claimed: prepatent time in numbers, egg counts, human infection, lice.
