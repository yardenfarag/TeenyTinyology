# Episode 001 — Pickle & Doodle (horsehair worm life cycle) — fact sheet

| # | Claim used in the script | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Horsehair worms (Nematomorpha) mate and lay eggs in water; eggs hatch into tiny larvae. | ✅ | [UC IPM – Horsehair worms](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/horsehair-worms/) · [UF/IFAS IN274](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IN274/pdf) |
| 2 | Larvae are swallowed by young aquatic insects (mayfly, mosquito, midge larvae) and form dormant cysts. | ✅ | [UC IPM](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/horsehair-worms/) · [Minnesota DNR](https://dnr.state.mn.us/mcvmagazine/issues/2017/jul-aug/horsehair-worm.html) |
| 3 | The insects carry the cysts through metamorphosis, fly to land as adults, die, and are scavenged by crickets/grasshoppers. | ✅ | [Minnesota DNR](https://dnr.state.mn.us/mcvmagazine/issues/2017/jul-aug/horsehair-worm.html) |
| 4 | Larvae must be swallowed — they cannot enter through the skin. | ✅ | [UF/IFAS IN274](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IN274/pdf) |
| 5 | Inside the host the worm grows to adult length, coiled up; it can be several times longer than the host. | ✅ | [UC IPM Pest Note](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/PMG/PESTNOTES/pn7471.html) · [Spinochordodes tellinii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinochordodes_tellinii) |
| 6 | Infected crickets behave erratically, get close to water and enter it (e.g. *Nemobius sylvestris* / *Paragordius tricuspidatus*). | ✅ | [Thomas et al. 2002 via PMC1559948](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1559948) |
| 7 | The exact mechanism is not fully known (light attraction is one hypothesis). | ✅ (worded as "still being worked out") | [Ponton et al. 2011, Behav. Ecol.](https://academic.oup.com/beheco/article/22/2/392/208177) |
| 8 | In water the worm leaves the host and swims off to find a mate. | ✅ | [UC IPM](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/horsehair-worms/) |
| 9 | The cricket can survive emergence if it doesn't drown; can live months afterwards. | ✅ | [Ponton et al. PMC3071748](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3071748) · [Parasitol. Res. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00436-021-07173-0) |
| 10 | Mayfly nymphs live in water; adults are short-lived. | ✅ (general knowledge, standard entomology) | — |
| 11 | Only male crickets chirp → Pickle (female) does not chirp. | ✅ | general entomology |

Not claimed (unverified): exact egg counts; exact worm length ratios for this species pair.

## Added with script v5 ("Is Doodle steering her? Scientists think so... but how he does it is still a mystery.")
- Manipulation is the mainstream view: infected crickets seek and enter water far more often than uninfected ones
  (Thomas et al. 2002, J. Evol. Biol.), and are drawn toward light reflected off water (Ponton et al. 2011, Behav. Ecol.).
- Mechanism not settled: proteomics shows altered host brain proteins and worm proteins resembling host signalling
  molecules (Biron et al. 2005, 2006), but the exact pathway is still unresolved.
